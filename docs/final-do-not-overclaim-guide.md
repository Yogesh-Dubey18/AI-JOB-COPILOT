# Final Do Not Overclaim Guide

Use this guide when writing README text, resumes, LinkedIn posts, portfolio pages, demo scripts, or recruiter messages.

## Safe Claims

- Built a full-stack AI job-search SaaS portfolio project.
- Implemented a Next.js frontend and Express TypeScript backend.
- Added MongoDB/Mongoose-ready persistence and local test fallback.
- Added JWT auth, resume workflows, job matching, application tracking, interview prep, analytics, admin, feedback, and documentation.
- Added provider-ready AI architecture with mock fallback.
- Added mock/Stripe-ready billing architecture.
- Added deployment runbooks for Vercel, Render/Railway/Fly.io, and MongoDB Atlas.
- Verified local builds and tests.

## Avoid These Claims Unless They Become True

- Do not say it has paying users.
- Do not say it has revenue.
- Do not say it is legally approved.
- Do not say it is production deployed unless live URLs are provided and verified.
- Do not say it is partnered with job boards or employers.
- Do not say it automatically applies to jobs.
- Do not say it guarantees interviews, offers, or selection.
- Do not say AI output is always accurate.

## Better Wording

- Say "provider-ready" instead of "fully integrated" when keys are not configured.
- Say "mock billing foundation" instead of "payments enabled" until Stripe is live.
- Say "deployment-ready structure" instead of "deployed" until live URLs are verified.
- Say "manual review workflow" instead of "auto-apply".
- Say "portfolio/demo release" instead of "commercial launch" until legal, billing, and support are ready.

## Resume Wording

Good:

> Built AI Job Copilot, a full-stack job-search SaaS demo with resume analysis, job matching, application tracking, mock AI provider fallback, admin tools, and deployment documentation.

Avoid:

> Launched a production AI hiring platform with real users and guaranteed job placement.
